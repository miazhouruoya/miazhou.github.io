Same Country, Different Decades

An AI-powered platform to measure, explain and predict gender opportunity gaps across education, health, jobs, infrastructure and unpaid work. This page walks through our slides and explains the logic and technical build behind each one.

01

Two questions, two measures

Framing · Data

Question

Gaps tell us who has less, not how long they wait. We turned inequality into two human units: years of delay (rural vs urban women) and hours of unpaid work (women vs men).

How we built it

  • Opportunity Delay: 7 World Bank datasets (WDI, Gender Statistics, Findex, SE4ALL, Labor Database, Jobs Indicators, ID4D).
  • 28 indicators across education, jobs, finance, health, infrastructure, digital and resilience.
  • Time Tax: UN SDG 5.4.1 time use + ILO participation + WDI controls.

Output

215 countries, 1990–2024, ~129,000 observations; 177 women–men time-use pairs across 89 countries.

02

Rebuild, measure, compare

Opportunity Delay · Part 1

Problem

Rural women are almost never measured directly, and 28 indicators sit on different scales. We need one comparable score per group, with uncertainty.

How we built it

  • Log-odds reconstruction of rural women from rural, female and total rates:
    logit⁡(XRW)=logit⁡(XR)+logit⁡(XF)−logit⁡(XT)\operatorname{logit}(X^{RW}) = \operatorname{logit}(X^{R}) + \operatorname{logit}(X^{F}) - \operatorname{logit}(X^{T})logit(XRW)=logit(XR)+logit(XF)−logit(XT)
  • Hierarchical Bayesian Dynamic Factor Model for four groups (RW, UW, RM, UM), fit with Gibbs sampling + Kalman filter (FFBS):
    yi,c,tg∼N(αi+λiZc,tg,σi2),Zc,tg∼N(ρZc,t−1g+δcg+γt,τ2)y^{g}_{i,c,t} \sim \mathcal{N}(\alpha_i + \lambda_i Z^{g}_{c,t}, \sigma_i^2),\quad Z^{g}_{c,t} \sim \mathcal{N}(\rho Z^{g}_{c,t-1} + \delta^{g}_{c} + \gamma_t, \tau^2)yi,c,tg​∼N(αi​+λi​Zc,tg​,σi2​),Zc,tg​∼N(ρZc,t−1g​+δcg​+γt​,τ2)
  • Trajectory matching: slide urban women's path back until it matches rural women's last five years.

Output

  • Reconstruction matched 1,263 direct surveys: r = 0.998, 1.3 points off.
  • 200 posterior draws, 90% intervals.
  • India: rural women ≈ urban women 13.5 years ago.
  • Median delays: education 17, health 12, infrastructure 16, jobs 21 years.
03

Is it closing, and who will fall behind?

Opportunity Delay · Part 2

Question

Improving is not the same as catching up. Is the gap shrinking, does education turn into jobs, and can we see widening gaps before they happen?

How we built it

  • Progress without convergence: posterior probability that rural women improve while the gap widens.
  • Bayesian half-life of the gap, pooled by region, plus a change-point test on yearly gap changes.
    log⁡Gc,t=ac−λct+ϵc,t,T1/2=ln⁡2/λc\log G_{c,t} = a_c - \lambda_c t + \epsilon_{c,t},\qquad T_{1/2} = \ln 2 / \lambda_clogGc,t​=ac​−λc​t+ϵc,t​,T1/2​=ln2/λc​
  • Hierarchical longitudinal model of jobs 3 years later on education.
  • Early warning: LightGBM + SHAP vs elastic-net, with time-split and leave-one-region-out tests.

Output

  • Gaps take ~27 years to halve; 10 countries progress without converging.
  • "Weak conversion" hypothesis rejected (P = 0.17): jobs simply arrive late.
  • Early warning AUC 0.87 vs 0.54 baseline.
  • Bangladesh: 18 → 6.5 years in a decade.
04

How long until rural kitchens catch up?

Infrastructure · Forecast

Question

Electricity, water and sanitation gaps are shrinking. Clean cooking is the only one still growing. When will rural areas reach today's urban level?

How we built it

  • Kalman local level + drift per country, drifts pooled within region.
  • First-passage time: first year a rural path reaches the urban 2024 level.
    Tc∗=inf⁡{h≥0:Zc,2024+hrural≥Zc,2024urban}T^*_c = \inf\{h \ge 0 : Z^{rural}_{c,2024+h} \ge Z^{urban}_{c,2024}\}Tc∗​=inf{h≥0:Zc,2024+hrural​≥Zc,2024urban​}
  • 4,000 simulated paths per country; map colour = first year with P ≥ 0.5.

Output

  • 61 of 114 economies unlikely to catch up before 2100.
  • 578M rural women still waiting in 2050.
  • Backtest (fit to 2014, forecast 2023) beats a no-change forecast for every service.
05

Is the gap itself closing, and what moves it?

Infrastructure · Drivers

Question

Which country conditions go with gaps closing faster or getting stuck?

How we built it

  • Gap model: each year a share κ of the gap closes, plus pushes from lagged covariates.
    Gc,t=Gc,t−1−κcGc,t−1+β′Xc,t−1+ηc,tG_{c,t} = G_{c,t-1} - \kappa_c G_{c,t-1} + \beta' X_{c,t-1} + \eta_{c,t}Gc,t​=Gc,t−1​−κc​Gc,t−1​+β′Xc,t−1​+ηc,t​
  • Hierarchical Bayes (Gibbs), κ pooled by region; report P(κ > 0).
  • Multinomial transitions between Converging, Stalled and Diverging, with a country-cluster bootstrap.

Output

  • Clean-cooking gap closing in 44% of economies, widening in 39%.
  • Closing sped up after 2012.
  • Fertility marks where gaps are hardest; internet where digital delivery can work. Associations, not levers.
06

Does freeing time expand opportunity?

Time Tax

Question

Women carry more unpaid work. Is that linked to less paid work, and when the burden falls, does participation rise?

How we built it

  • Time Tax = women's minus men's unpaid hours per day.
    Tc,t=Hc,twomen−Hc,tmenT_{c,t} = H^{women}_{c,t} - H^{men}_{c,t}Tc,t​=Hc,twomen​−Hc,tmen​
  • Pearson / Spearman / partial correlations with female participation and the gender gap, controlling for GDP, year, fertility, urbanization.
  • Freed-time test on first vs last surveys:
    Fc=60 (Hfirstwomen−Hlastwomen)F_c = 60\,(H^{women}_{first} - H^{women}_{last})Fc​=60(Hfirstwomen​−Hlastwomen​)

Output

  • Women do more unpaid work in every country; median gap 2.46 h/day.
  • r = −0.58 with female participation; +0.68 with the gender gap.
  • One-third of countries freeing 10+ min/day saw no participation gain.
07

How the multi-agent workflow uses the results

Architecture

Principle

Statistics find the pattern; AI explains it. The language model never computes a number. It only retrieves our precomputed findings and cites sources.

How we built it

  • Offline engine: ~2,050 structured findings + a futures grid (215 economies × 5 services × 3 targets × 4 years) as CSV/GZIP/JSON.
  • Frontend: React 19 + Vite 8, MapLibre GL JS map, country dashboards, chat.
  • Backend: Python server + LangGraph StateGraph with a shared TypedDict state; OpenRouter for models, Tavily for search.
  • 5 agents: Understand & Locate → Statistics ∥ Context Search → Futures → Report.

Guardrails

  • JSON schema check and citation-ID check against retrieved sources.
  • No probabilities, no causal claims.
  • Fertility used only as a positioning signal.
  • Each phase streams live as NDJSON.
08

The Data Assistant in action

Product

Flow

The user picks a starter question or types one, e.g. "Please explain the opportunity gaps of Armenia."

What happens

  • Locate: the assistant finds Armenia and the map flies to it in 3D.
  • Statistics ∥ Web context: local model records and Tavily sources load in parallel.
  • Research brief: a written report with evidence tags that link back to the model or the web sources.

Result

Armenia's rural gap persists despite gains: urban women 0.62 units ahead, delay 22.2 years (90% CI 18.2–31.0), with every claim traceable.

10

From what we found to what can be done

Conclusion

Jobs arrive last

Schooling reaches rural women first, services next, paid work last. → The delay map shows which step lags, so funding targets rural jobs.

Improving ≠ catching up

Many gaps stall because cities move faster; clean cooking is still widening. → Early warning and the Futures agent flag where delays may grow, and how fast a country must move.

Freed time ≠ opportunity

Less unpaid work often brings no gain in paid work. → The evidence agent finds what worked elsewhere, like childcare and transport, with sources for and against.
